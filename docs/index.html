Why the foundation model wins only some series
Chronos-Bolt forecasts zero-shot, so it contributes most where history is shortest. It took 5 of 22 series on lowest MASE — Current account balance (+22%), Prime lending rate (+38%), Labour force participation (+9%), Policy rate (+30%), Real effective exchange rate (+28%). On long, well-sampled monthly series such as consumer inflation (800+ observations) classical ETS still wins, because there is enough data for seasonal structure to be estimated directly rather than borrowed from a prior. Every result shown here comes from the same rolling-origin backtest, so selection is decided by measured accuracy, not preference.

The Power BI bundle uses a single long fact table with series_key as the foreign key rather than one table per indicator — 29 near-identical tables would be unusable in a field list. The mapping is lossless; every indicator remains individually selectable.

Sources

All key-free and read-only. Locally cached for 12 hours.

South African Reserve Bank primary

Official Web API. Policy and prime rates, bond yields, the rand, real effective exchange rate, CPI and PPI, retail and manufacturing volumes, mining output, vehicle sales and the leading indicator. Updated within days of each release.

Stats SA (via SARB)

Consumer and producer inflation originate at Statistics South Africa; both are carried on the SARB Web API, which is how this dashboard gets them without scraping the Stats SA site.

World Bank Open Data

Annual macroeconomic, labour, fiscal and social indicators. Published under CC BY 4.0.

IMF — IFS & WEO

Long-run history and published projections to 2030, via the DBnomics mirror.

FRED

Quarterly real GDP from the Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis.

Recency varies by indicator, and the table below always states each series' last observed period. Rate, rand and inflation data are current to within a few weeks. Annual indicators sourced from the World Bank — GDP growth, unemployment, population, the debt ratio — reflect the latest published annual figure, which is inherently a year or more behind. Treat an annual number as a structural benchmark, not a current reading.
